01Summary

The verdict in one screen

DC is missing lifestyle photography on its product pages. On the PDP, the shopper gets a warm-grey studio, a cropped waist-down model and fifteen near-identical frames: nothing shows where or how the product is worn.

The brands that win with 16–25 year olds do the opposite: the first image is already a world (a Madrid wall, a New York crosswalk, a snowy park), the packshot comes second, and the page sells the outfit, not the item.

  1. Put lifestyle inside the gallery. 2–4 location images per product, shot for the product pages.
  2. Add a 6–13 s product video in slot 2, like Beyond Medals (4:5, model turning, full fit visible).
  3. Show fit, not just size. Model height and size under the price, not buried in the specs accordion.
  4. Sell the look. A “Complete the look” row (EME, Nude, Cold Culture, Scuffers all have one).
  5. Make snow tech visual. Feature photos + pictograms (Beyond Medals) instead of a bullet list.
  6. One art direction per world. Studio, street, mountain. Same light and grade across the catalogue.

Not enough lifestyle on the product pages

Homepage

Works

  • Clear seasonality (snow, winter boots) and gender entry points.
  • Category shortcuts (shoes, sweats, tees, pants).

Hurts

  • A contest bar (“Gagnez un snow trip aux 2 Alpes”) eats the very first line.
  • Typography is generic (system sans, all-caps white boxes), so the page reads “mall brand”, not “skate brand”.
  • The homepage is the only place with any outdoor imagery; it never reaches the product pages.

Two issues: product tiles are all cropped studio shots on the same warm grey, and the hover/second image is never a lifestyle shot.

Video inside the product gallery

Why it matters

For a baggy snow pant, a still photo cannot show how the fabric falls over a boot or moves when the rider turns. Beyond Medals solves it with one short loop per product, shot in the same studio as the stills.

DC has no video on any of the four product pages audited. The two GIF recordings of Beyond Medals were also saved to the Downloads folder of the capture Mac.

Four vibes in the panel

1 · Clean studio, human poses

Suspicious, EME, Two Jeys. Pale grey, soft light, but loose real poses and two genders. DC's current lane, minus the life in the poses.

2 · Street documentary

Nude Project, Scuffers. Daylight, walls, crosswalks, shop counters. The model looks caught, not posed. Most aspirational for DC streetwear and denim.

3 · Flash & night

Cold Culture, GODS. Direct flash, glossy skin, nightlife. Strong for drops and outerwear launches.

4 · Mountain, rider-made

Beyond Medals, Drink Sexy. Real riders, fisheye, heavy grades, retro interiors. The natural lane for DC Snow, which has the riders and the history.

Where DC shows the product outdoors: only on the homepage

On the product page itself, the shopper only ever sees the grey studio. DC needs new lifestyle imagery made for the galleries: phone-shot, street and mountain, in today's vibe (see “Shot on iPhone, on purpose”).
06Lifestyle shot inspirations

What their lifestyle photos actually look like

The lifestyle images competitors put inside their product galleries, at a size where you can read the light, the place and the attitude. These are the references for DC's next shoot.

Nude Project

Street documentary, overcast daylight

Grey sky, real walls, real streets. The model is caught walking, thinking, turning away. Nobody smiles at the camera. Styling mixes the puffer with tailored trousers or washed denim, so the product reads as part of a wardrobe. Two images in this gallery still carry iPhone file names (IMG_4099, IMG_3573).

Scuffers

Selfie & candid, white-wall studio + corridor

Half catalogue, half camera roll. The selfie literally shows the phone, which tells the shopper “this is how it looks on someone like you.” File names IMG_8843_SCFF and IMG_5296_SCFF are straight from an iPhone.

GODS

London, flash, public transport

Every frame has a city cue: the tube doors, a black railing, wet brick. The flash shot makes the waterproof shell look real (you see the drops). This is how a 3-layer jacket sells to someone who will wear it on the way to class, not on a glacier.

Beyond Medals

Rider-made, snow at dusk, van life

Shot by and with riders: the van, the parking lot, the trick, the flash in falling snow. The studio frame is there for fit; everything else is the day around it. Two gallery files are literally named “skärmavbild” (Swedish for screenshot): frames grabbed from video or a phone.

Cold Culture

Studio, but with the set left in

The last image of the gallery is a phone photo taken on set, with the lighting rig still visible. It costs nothing (the shoot is already happening) and it makes a glossy studio product feel like a moment.

EME Studios · Drink Sexy

Homepage worlds

Both keep their product pages simple and put the full world on the homepage and Instagram. Drink Sexy goes furthest: over-filtered, fisheye, deliberately imperfect, like a rider's own edit.

07DC vs them

The vibe gap, image by image

Side by side, left is DC, right is a competitor, with what changes in the vibe.

Same category, same price band. DC: centred, waist-up, goggles on in a studio, warm-grey seamless, even softbox. GODS: a real tube carriage, the model busy zipping up, mixed fluorescent light. One is a catalogue entry, the other is a moment you could be in.

DC shows the baggy fit from the waist down on grey, with red-and-white sneakers. Beyond Medals shows the same silhouette in deep snow with direct flash and snow on the fabric: the waterproofing is proven, not stated.

DC's own campaign is already outdoors, but it is a polished hero: blue sky, posed, retouched, an ad. Beyond Medals' is documentary: overcast, a van, a rider doing something ordinary. Gen Z reads the second as more credible.

DC: headless, centred, white tee, neon sneakers, grey void. Scuffers: a mirror selfie, the phone in hand, a roller shutter and a chrome chair. Same baggy jean silhouette, completely different promise.

Both are product-only, both outdoors. DC: saturated, glossy, perfectly lit still life on a fence. Nude: the jacket dropped on the pavement, shot from above, the photographer's own shoes in frame. Polish versus point-of-view.

Why it works

  • Speed: one day, three locations, a whole collection covered.
  • Cost: no location permits, no lighting crew, no retouching per frame.
  • Native look: it looks like the feed the customer scrolls on Instagram and TikTok.
  • Trust: imperfect light and real places read as honest.
  • Reuse: the same frames feed product pages, stories, ads and emails.

What stays in the studio

  • Colour-accurate packshots (returns depend on it).
  • Front/back/side fit shots and details.
  • Hero campaign images for the homepage.

A “phone day” for DC: one collection, one day

Team

  1. 1 photographer with a recent iPhone Pro (main lens + flash)
  2. 2–4 models: riders, skaters, staff, friends
  3. 1 stylist with the full drop

Places

  1. A street or skate spot (concrete, shutters, walls)
  2. An interior: metro, café, corridor, car park
  3. Snow: resort car park, lift, van, at dusk with flash

Per product

  1. 1 full-body walking shot
  2. 1 back or three-quarter view
  3. 1 close detail in situation
  4. 1 vertical 6–10 s clip

Consistency

  1. One shared editing preset per season
  2. Same crop ratio (4:5) for galleries
  3. Model releases and a naming convention
  4. Two to four phone shots per gallery, never all of it

Where DC is ahead

  • Customer reviews on the page (no streetwear competitor shows them).
  • Delivery date, store availability, pay-in-4.
  • Colour variants as photo thumbnails.

11Homepages

First screen on a phone

Homepage video, recorded on iPhone (390 px)

The first screen moves. Cold Culture and Scuffers open on a full-screen looping film; Nude Project and Beyond Medals put video further down the page. None of these films look like an advert: they are short, raw, phone-paced clips with one idea each (a snowman, a supermarket shelf, a street crossing). Several also name the drop (“No. 57”, “Winter Drop 2”, “New drop live now”). DC's mobile hero is framed with a contest banner, generic type and white-box buttons. The fix: one looping 6–10 s clip per drop, a drop name in a brand display face, and one call to action.

Three art directions, one per world

DC Studio

Cool concrete-grey seamless (not warm beige), harder side light, full-body crops with shoes visible, natural poses. Used for slots 3–6 on every product.

DC Street

Daylight, Paris/Barcelona walls, skate spots, shop counters. Candid, slight grain. Used for image 1 on footwear, denim, tees.

DC Mountain

The Trackstar campaign look: sun, snow, real riders, a touch of fisheye. Used for image 1 and 5 on all snow products.

Producing it without reshooting everything

Shoot new, fast

  • A phone lifestyle day per drop: street, interior, snow (see the iPhone section).
  • Shoot the turning video on the next e-com day: same set, one extra camera.

Then generate the gaps

  • Nightjar: save one “photography style” per world from three reference images, then place each packshot in that world with consistent AI models.
  • Photoroom batch (up to 250 images): same scene on a whole category; similar but not identical backgrounds, so use a fixed background image when you need them identical.
  • Check every logo, seam and colour; label AI images as concepts until validated.

Roadmap

0–1 month
  • First phone lifestyle day, top 20 products
  • Fit line under price
  • Fix “Confort NaN”
  • Cross-sell jacket ↔ pant
1–3 months
  • Video on top 30 products
  • Snow pictogram row + feature cards
  • Complete the look
  • Low-stock per size
3–6 months
  • New studio + street art direction
  • Homepage drop structure + display face
  • UGC from Instagram tags
  • A/B test image 1 = lifestyle vs studio
