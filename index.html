A mobile benchmark of product pages, imagery and lifestyle photography across 10 sites: DC, seven Spanish/Belgian streetwear labels and two baggy snow brands. Every screen below was captured on an iPhone 12 Pro viewport (390 × 844) on 16–17 November 2026.

01Summary

The verdict in one screen

DC is missing lifestyle photography on its product pages. On the PDP, the shopper gets a warm-grey studio, a cropped waist-down model and fifteen near-identical frames: nothing shows where or how the product is worn.

The brands that win with 16–25 year olds do the opposite: the first image is already a world (a Madrid wall, a New York crosswalk, a snowy park), the packshot comes second, and the page sells the outfit, not the item.

  1. Put lifestyle inside the gallery. 2–4 location images per product, shot for the product pages.
  2. Add a 6–13 s product video in slot 2, like Beyond Medals (4:5, model turning, full fit visible).
  3. Show fit, not just size. Model height and size under the price, not buried in the specs accordion.
  4. Sell the look. A “Complete the look” row (EME, Nude, Cold Culture, Scuffers all have one).
  5. Make snow tech visual. Feature photos + pictograms (Beyond Medals) instead of a bullet list.
  6. One art direction per world. Studio, street, mountain. Same light and grade across the catalogue.

Works

Four vibes in the panel

1 · Clean studio, human poses

Suspicious, EME, Two Jeys. Pale grey, soft light, but loose real poses and two genders. DC's current lane, minus the life in the poses.

2 · Street documentary

Nude Project, Scuffers. Daylight, walls, crosswalks, shop counters. The model looks caught, not posed. Most aspirational for DC streetwear and denim.

3 · Flash & night

Cold Culture, GODS. Direct flash, glossy skin, nightlife. Strong for drops and outerwear launches.

4 · Mountain, rider-made

Beyond Medals, Drink Sexy. Real riders, fisheye, heavy grades, retro interiors. The natural lane for DC Snow, which has the riders and the history.

Where DC shows the product outdoors: only on the homepage

On the product page itself, the shopper only ever sees the grey studio. DC needs new lifestyle imagery made for the galleries: phone-shot, street and mountain, in today's vibe (see “Shot on iPhone, on purpose”).

Why it works

  • Speed: one day, three locations, a whole collection covered.
  • Cost: no location permits, no lighting crew, no retouching per frame.
  • Native look: it looks like the feed the customer scrolls on Instagram and TikTok.
  • Trust: imperfect light and real places read as honest.
  • Reuse: the same frames feed product pages, stories, ads and emails.

What stays in the studio

  • Colour-accurate packshots (returns depend on it).
  • Front/back/side fit shots and details.
  • Hero campaign images for the homepage.

A “phone day” for DC: one collection, one day

Team

  1. 1 photographer with a recent iPhone Pro (main lens + flash)
  2. 2–4 models: riders, skaters, staff, friends
  3. 1 stylist with the full drop

Places

  1. A street or skate spot (concrete, shutters, walls)
  2. An interior: metro, café, corridor, car park
  3. Snow: resort car park, lift, van, at dusk with flash

Per product

  1. 1 full-body walking shot
  2. 1 back or three-quarter view
  3. 1 close detail in situation
  4. 1 vertical 6–10 s clip

Consistency

  1. One shared editing preset per season
  2. Same crop ratio (4:5) for galleries
  3. Model releases and a naming convention
  4. Two to four phone shots per gallery, never all of it

Where DC is ahead

  • Customer reviews on the page (no streetwear competitor shows them).
  • Delivery date, store availability, pay-in-4.
  • Colour variants as photo thumbnails.

Where DC is behind

  • No lifestyle, no video, no outfit.

  • No urgency: EME flags “Dernières unités” per size.

Three art directions, one per world

DC Studio

Cool concrete-grey seamless (not warm beige), harder side light, full-body crops with shoes visible, natural poses. Used for slots 3–6 on every product.

DC Street

Daylight, Paris/Barcelona walls, skate spots, shop counters. Candid, slight grain. Used for image 1 on footwear, denim, tees.

DC Mountain

The Trackstar campaign look: sun, snow, real riders, a touch of fisheye. Used for image 1 and 5 on all snow products.

Producing it without reshooting everything

Shoot new, fast

  • A phone lifestyle day per drop: street, interior, snow (see the iPhone section).
  • Shoot the turning video on the next e-com day: same set, one extra camera.

Then generate the gaps

  • Nightjar: save one “photography style” per world from three reference images, then place each packshot in that world with consistent AI models.
  • Photoroom batch (up to 250 images): same scene on a whole category; similar but not identical backgrounds, so use a fixed background image when you need them identical.
  • Check every logo, seam and colour; label AI images as concepts until validated.

What changed, and why

Homepage

  • First screen is a drop, not a contest: a name (“Ice Age”), a date, one button. A looping film plays behind it (scroll video stands in for it here).
  • Category bubbles under the hero, like Instagram stories, so the phone user is one tap from Puffers, Snow or Shoes.
  • Product grid opens on the lifestyle image, the packshot appears on swipe or hover.
  • One editorial block with attitude (grill, projection on a building) instead of white tiles.
  • “Shop the look” with hotspots and a one-tap “Add the full look”.
  • Free shipping, returns, Klarna and the DC Crew programme said once, clearly, above the footer.

Product page

  • Image 1 is shot on location, image 2 is a 6–13 s video (badge “Video · 0:13”), studio and details come after.
  • A one-line pitch under the price that sounds like the brand, not a spec sheet.
  • Colour as photo swatches, sizes as big chips with low-stock dots.
  • Fit block under the sizes: model height and size, plus a slim → oversized slider.
  • Delivery promise with a date, free returns in green, Klarna under the price.
  • Tech as four pictograms (10K, 650 fill, −20°, recycled) and feature photo cards, the long bullet list moves into an accordion.
  • “Complete the look” on black with one button for the whole outfit, reviews with customer photos and fit feedback (“runs large 71 %”).
